# Mini-manuscript

## Background

Telephone follow-up may support medication adherence after hospital discharge, but evidence in older adults living alone is limited.

## Methods

We conducted a parallel, individually randomized trial at two hospitals. Adults aged 70 years or older who lived alone were assigned to a pharmacist telephone call 72 hours after discharge or usual care. The primary outcome was the proportion taking all prescribed medicines at 30 days. Outcome assessors were blinded. Follow-up lasted 30 days.

## Results

We randomized 160 participants: 81 to telephone follow-up and 79 to usual care. At 30 days, 58 of 81 participants (71.6%) in the intervention group and 49 of 79 (62.0%) in the control group were taking all prescribed medicines (risk difference, 9.6 percentage points; 95% CI, -4.6 to 23.8). No other outcome is reported here.

## Discussion

Telephone follow-up was associated with a higher observed adherence proportion, but the confidence interval included no difference and clinically important benefit. The small two-center trial did not establish efficacy.
