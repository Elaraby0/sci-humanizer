## Background

It is important to note that symptom scores may inform follow-up decisions.
The objective of this study was to compare three scores in a validation sample.

## Methods

The observational study included 240 adults. Blood pressure and pulse were
recorded simultaneously at 08:00. The analysis used `model_v3 --complete-cases`
as specified in the [analysis plan](https://example.org/protocol-v3?mode=full).

## Results

Score A ranked first, score B second, and score C third for discrimination in
the validation sample. The three prespecified endpoints were pain (42 events),
fatigue (58 events), and sleep disturbance (31 events). That is the real win.

## Discussion

The observational design limits causal interpretation. Residual confounding
may explain the association. These groundbreaking findings underscore the
transformative importance of measuring symptoms.

## Sensitivity analysis

It should be emphasized that the complete-case analysis gave an adjusted odds
ratio of 1.22 (95% CI 0.94–1.58). The interval includes no association. That
distinction matters.
