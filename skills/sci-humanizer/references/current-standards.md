# Current standards and policy anchors

This reference supplements the Stanford course where publication policy has
changed or where the course intentionally gave only broad advice. Policies are
time-sensitive. Check the target journal, funder, institution, and current
official source at the time of use.

Last checked: 2026-08-30.

## Priority order

1. Applicable law, research-ethics approval, and institutional policy.
2. Current target journal, funder, or assignment instructions.
3. Current study-specific reporting guideline.
4. Current discipline-wide integrity and authorship guidance.
5. SciHumanizer's course-derived writing defaults.

Never claim full compliance merely because the prose was edited. A reporting
checklist assesses reporting completeness, not necessarily study quality.

## Reporting guidelines

Use the [EQUATOR Network reporting-guideline
library](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/) to identify the
current guideline and relevant extension.

Common mappings include:

| Study or document | Common guideline family |
|---|---|
| Randomized trial | CONSORT and relevant extension |
| Trial protocol | SPIRIT and relevant extension |
| Observational study | STROBE and relevant extension |
| Systematic review | PRISMA and relevant extension |
| Systematic-review protocol | PRISMA-P |
| Diagnostic-accuracy study | STARD |
| Prediction-model study | TRIPOD and relevant extension |
| Case report | CARE |
| Qualitative research | COREQ or SRQR, as appropriate |
| Animal research | ARRIVE |
| Quality-improvement study | SQUIRE |
| Economic evaluation | CHEERS |
| Clinical practice guideline | AGREE or RIGHT, as appropriate |

Do not assume the base guideline is sufficient; topic, intervention, design,
and data type may have an extension. Follow the latest official version, such
as CONSORT 2025 rather than an older checklist when applicable.

## Authorship

For biomedical journals, consult the current [ICMJE authorship and contributor
recommendations](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html).
ICMJE currently requires all four of these conditions:

1. substantial contribution to conception or design, or acquisition, analysis,
   or interpretation of data;
2. drafting the work or critically revising important intellectual content;
3. final approval of the version to be published; and
4. accountability for the work's accuracy and integrity.

Activities such as funding acquisition, general supervision, administrative
support, language editing, or proofreading do not alone establish authorship.
People who satisfy the first criterion should have the opportunity to
participate in drafting, review, and final approval rather than being prevented
from qualifying.

Authorship order varies by discipline and must be agreed by the group. Do not
embed the course's biomedical first-author/last-author convention as a
universal rule.

## Contributorship

Use the [CRediT contributor-role taxonomy](https://credit.niso.org/) when the
venue requests or accepts it. Its 14 roles describe conceptualization, data
curation, formal analysis, funding acquisition, investigation, methodology,
project administration, resources, software, supervision, validation,
visualization, writing of the original draft, and writing through review and
editing.

CRediT describes contributions; it does not replace the venue's authorship
criteria.

## AI-assisted writing

Consult the current [ICMJE guidance on AI in
publishing](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/).
The current principles include:

- do not list or cite an AI system as an author;
- human authors remain responsible for accuracy, integrity, originality,
  permissions, and appropriate attribution;
- disclose whether and how AI-assisted technology was used when the venue
  requires it;
- do not treat AI output as a primary source; and
- preserve the confidentiality of submitted manuscripts and peer review.

For a submission, inspect the journal's own policy because disclosure location
and wording vary.

## Publication ethics and text reuse

Use current resources from the [Committee on Publication Ethics
(COPE)](https://publicationethics.org/) and the target venue for plagiarism,
text recycling, redundant publication, authorship disputes, corrections,
retractions, peer review, and conflicts of interest.

Do not copy a course example or a prior publication into a manuscript simply
because it is available. Quote and attribute exact language; synthesize source
ideas independently; obtain permissions where required.

## Predatory or deceptive journals

The course's specific lists and counts are historical. Evaluate a journal using
current evidence:

- transparent ownership, editorial board, fees, peer review, and policies;
- verifiable indexing and metrics;
- a publication record consistent with its stated scope;
- clear contact information and archiving; and
- current institutional or recognized directory guidance.

Do not label a journal predatory from one superficial sign. Do not treat an
invented `impact factor` or unsolicited invitation as evidence of legitimacy.

## Venue and funder instructions

Before finalizing a submission, check:

- article type and scope;
- word, abstract, reference, table, and figure limits;
- required headings and reporting checklist;
- data, code, materials, protocol, and registration statements;
- ethics, consent, funding, conflict, contributorship, and AI disclosures;
- citation and reference format;
- figure resolution, color, accessibility, and file requirements; and
- whether tracked changes, clean copies, response letters, or cover letters are
  required.

Do not guess a venue's format from memory. A recently published article can
illustrate house style, but official current author instructions control.
